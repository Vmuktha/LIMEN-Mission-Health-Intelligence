# Limitations and responsible-use boundaries

- LIMEN is a research prototype, not a clinical diagnostic, fitness-for-duty determination, or medical device.
- A mouse/trackpad task is not equivalent to a joystick-based laboratory experiment or a complete published protocol.
- Behavioural cursor data cannot establish brain activation, vestibular function, or underlying neural mechanisms.
- Shortened protocols may be useful for demonstration only until separately validated.
- Within-person baseline comparison does not eliminate confounding from fatigue, hardware, environment, instruction adherence, practice, injury, or other factors.
- Incomplete sessions and low valid-trial counts must be flagged rather than interpreted as evidence of recovery or impairment.
- The pre-v0.2.0 task assumed that the task-area centre was the physical pointer origin. Its origin-sensitive metrics are retained only as origin-unverified historical records and are not comparable with corrected sessions. Any record without a finite captured actual start and positioning timestamp is treated the same way.
- The v0.2.0/v0.3.0 positioning tolerance (12 CSS pixels), dwell (250 ms), and movement-onset displacement threshold (3 CSS pixels) are deterministic prototype rules, not clinically or experimentally validated thresholds. They require piloting across devices and display configurations.
- Protocol v0.3.0 hides the native browser cursor during active interaction. Hiding the system pointer is a measurement-protocol change, so v0.2.0 and v0.3.0 results should not be pooled or directly compared for origin-sensitive metrics without an explicit compatibility justification. Historical sessions are preserved but are not rewritten.
- Pausing during an active target invalidates that trial rather than stopping and resuming its clock. This avoids contaminating reaction time, movement time, or the timeout with paused time, but it can reduce the number of usable trials and may introduce selection effects if pausing is frequent.
- Reaction and movement times remain available for legacy/mixed sessions when timestamps are valid, but that does not make their origin-sensitive metrics valid or comparable.
- The directional-error metric is the first raw movement sample at least 3 CSS pixels from the captured start. It is not an error at peak velocity, and the correction-behaviour peak-velocity time is a separate geometric descriptor.
- Recovery observations are descriptive analysis products, not clinical recovery determinations, fitness-for-duty conclusions, or readiness scores. A Personal Reference Session is an explicit comparison reference, not a clinical baseline.
- A completed assessment session is not automatically a valid reference session. Eligibility is metric-specific: timing metrics and origin-sensitive metrics have different requirements, and adaptation or washout values additionally require eligible samples in their trial windows.
- Recovery comparisons require identical measurement protocol versions. v0.2.0 and v0.3.0 records are never pooled, including for timing metrics. Unknown, mixed, or inconsistent protocols make the affected comparison unavailable rather than silently comparable.
- Recovery deltas are `currentValue − referenceValue` and retain their sign. LIMEN does not label a change as improvement or deterioration and applies no arbitrary recovery thresholds.
- Missing recovery values remain null. LIMEN does not interpolate, zero-impute, or calculate a composite recovery score. Low eligible-trial counts and non-finite or malformed records are exposed as data-quality limitations rather than treated as evidence.
- The recovery dataset uses one browser-local participant profile and the existing participant ID. It is not a verified human identity, multi-participant account system, or cross-device record. Check-ins are associated only through an explicit completed-session identifier; timestamp proximity is never used.
- The `/recovery` dashboard is a descriptive view of the same browser-local records, not a clinically validated recovery dashboard. It has no readiness score, discrepancy score, recovery threshold, prediction, or diagnosis.
- Performance–self-report alignment is a co-observation of mathematical directions. It does not establish causation, improvement, deterioration, symptom validity, or a relationship between a task metric and a subjective state. Rows with a missing answer or a one-sided change are reported as insufficient data rather than assigned a same/opposite category.
- Longitudinal lines are visual guides only. Separate measurement protocols are never connected, missing observations remain gaps, and no interpolation or zero fill is applied. Sparse observations can make a visual trend appear more continuous than the underlying evidence supports.
- The dashboard reads local IndexedDB records in the current browser profile. Private browsing, storage policy, eviction, unavailable storage, or clearing site data can make observations inaccessible; the interface reports this rather than substituting sample data.
- Legacy records without a per-trial measurement label and records with an unrecognized protocol have no protocol-compatible reference session, so they cannot be compared with any observation, including other legacy records. The dashboard states this rather than proposing a session that cannot be used.
- A browser-local profile that stores more than one participant identifier is reported as ambiguous and is not merged or resolved automatically. Only one profile is shown, and no multi-participant management is provided.
- Recovery timestamps are displayed in UTC. A reader comparing them with local notes must convert them explicitly.
- NASA research and standards provide context; they do not imply NASA endorsement, operational authorization, or access to NASA datasets.

## Stage 4 domain boundaries

- Mission phases are a closed vocabulary recorded explicitly by a user. LIMEN never infers a phase from a timestamp, date, elapsed time, or mission schedule. The legacy values `inflight`, `postflight`, and `reconditioning` remain readable in historical records but are not accepted for new mission contexts or phase changes.
- Mission events record a user-supplied `occurredAt` and an optional explicit phase. The event timestamp is never used to derive its phase.
- The expanded check-in adds sleep quality, sleep-schedule disruption, workload, stress, general wellbeing, and existing symptoms to the original six items. Each item declares a polarity; opposite-polarity answers are never averaged, reversed into a single score, or treated as a diagnosis. An unanswered item remains absent, never zero.
- Free-text check-in context is optional, trimmed, bounded at 1,000 characters, stored locally, and not analysed, summarised, classified, or transmitted.
- Signal Concordance compares only explicitly linked sessions and check-ins. Timestamp proximity is never used to construct an association. The engine reports per-metric and per-field directions plus direction-only links; it produces no composite health, readiness, recovery, or risk score, no diagnosis, no prediction, and no causal claim.
- A `co-occurrence` link means two signed directions have the same mathematical sign. It does not mean the measures agree in valence, measure the same construct, or establish a relationship.
- Measurement protocol v0.2.0 and v0.3.0 are never pooled or directly compared, even for timing metrics. Differing assessment protocol versions are also incompatible.
- A metric without an eligible, protocol-compatible reference is reported as unavailable or insufficient-reference. It is never imputed, zero-filled, or replaced by a nearby observation.
- Mission-phase context in Signal Concordance comes only from an explicitly recorded check-in phase or an explicit mission context. Conflicting explicit records are reported as conflicting; LIMEN does not choose between them.
- Follow-up records are administrative workflow notes. They are not medical advice, a diagnosis, a prescription, or a clinical queue. A referral is a user-entered request for authorised review, with `performedByLimen` always `false`; LIMEN does not contact a clinician or transmit records.
- Evidence records are structurally separated into published findings, NASA study-catalogue metadata, downloadable NASA measurement data, user-generated observations, and synthetic demonstration scenarios. A catalogue record is never presented as a downloadable dataset, and no publication, finding, participant measurement, or availability status is fabricated.
- The OSDR reference layer contains transformed, four-person, ground-only pre/post-flight biospecimen measurements. It has no in-flight sample and no control arm. Reference ranges are source laboratory intervals, not clinical decision limits, and are never evaluated. Serum and whole-blood measurements remain distinct and are never joined to browser assessments, check-ins, mission events, or follow-up records.
- IndexedDB version 2 is an additive upgrade. Existing assessment and check-in stores are preserved; new mission, event, and follow-up stores are created only when missing. There is no destructive migration, backend, account system, network service, or cross-device synchronization. Records remain subject to browser storage policy, private browsing, eviction, and site-data clearing.
